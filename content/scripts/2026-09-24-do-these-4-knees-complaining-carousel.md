## 2. SCRIPT (8 slides)

1. DO THESE 4. "Do these 4 every day this week when your knees are complaining."
2. ONE. "Doorframe Row. 8 reps. Grip the frame, lean back, pull your chest to your hands."
3. TWO. "Wall Push-Up. 10 reps. Hands on the wall, lower your chest, press yourself back out."
4. THREE. "Glute Bridge. 10 reps. Lie on your back, feet flat, push your hips up."
5. FOUR. "Lying Heel Tap. 10 reps. Knees bent above your hips, tap one heel down and back."
6. WHAT THIS SKIPS. "No squat pattern this week. This is training what is fresh, not treatment."
7. THE STRUCTURE. "Two rounds of the four. Eight minutes, and nothing bends a knee under load."
8. YOUR TURN. "Which one are you starting with?"

## 3. VISUALS / SCENE BREAKDOWN

Typography only, no footage. Slides two, three and four carry the authored
figure at the peak pose (`figure: true`), because Doorframe Row, Wall Push-Up
and Glute Bridge all have approved exports in `assets/animation-library.json`.
Lying Heel Tap has no export yet, so slide five is type only.

Slide six is the honest slide. It names the gap out loud: there is no squat
pattern in this list, the list trains what is fresh rather than the joint that
is complaining, and the squat comes back when the knees stop complaining.
Nothing here treats, relieves or protects anything, and the deck says so in
plain words instead of implying otherwise.

Every movement is in `movement-library.md` at its library dose: Doorframe Row
(pull, tier 3, wall, 8 reps), Wall Push-Up (push, tier 1, wall, 10 reps),
Glute Bridge (hinge, tier 1, 10 reps), Lying Heel Tap (core, tier 2, 10 reps).
Three of the four are done standing still or lying down; none of them bends a
knee under load, which is the whole reason the four sit together.

## 4. CAPTION AND HASHTAGS

No squat workout for the days your knees are complaining: Doorframe Row, Wall Push-Up, Glute Bridge, Lying Heel Tap. Two rounds of the four, eight minutes, and nothing that bends a knee under load. Save this for the day the stairs are talking to you.
