# Sore is not the proof (carousel)

- Topic: The session counts whether or not anything aches tomorrow, and the proof she can count instead.
- Pillar: Reframe
- Format: slideshow (8 slides, TikTok photo post)
- Posting date: 2026-09-29
- Target search phrase: do you have to be sore for a workout to work

## 1. HOOK (first 3 seconds)

"Nobody tells you this: sore the next day is not proof it worked."
(contradiction: owner format #39, reveal the missing fact. It contradicts the
belief most viewers already hold, and the deck resolves it with a test she
can run herself.)

## 2. SCRIPT (8 slides)

1. NOBODY TELLS YOU. "Nobody tells you this: sore the next day is not proof it worked."
2. THE MYTH. "If you are not sore tomorrow, it did not count. That is the myth."
3. WHAT SORE MEANS. "Sore mostly means new. Not better, not harder. Just new to you."
4. WHAT KEEPS WORKING. "Do the same ten minutes every day. It stops feeling sore. It keeps working."
5. THE REAL PROOF. "The proof is your reps going up and the stairs getting easier."
6. TRY THIS. "Sit-to-Stand. Count your reps in one minute today. Count again in two weeks."
7. REMEMBER THIS. "Sore tomorrow or not, today's ten minutes counted. You built that."
8. YOUR TURN. "How many did you get today?"

## 3. VISUALS / SCENE BREAKDOWN

Typography only, no footage. Poster cover, then six plain cards that walk
from the myth to a test. The only movement named is Sit-to-Stand (squat,
tier 2, chair), used as a count test rather than a dosed set, so the slide
gives the test window instead of the library's 8 reps. It has no approved

No medical explanation anywhere: nothing about muscle damage, recovery,

Two capability lines: "You count it. Nobody else has to." and "You built

## 4. CAPTION AND HASHTAGS

Do you have to be sore for a workout to work? No: sore mostly means new, and the real proof is your reps going up and the stairs getting easier. Save this and count again in two weeks.
