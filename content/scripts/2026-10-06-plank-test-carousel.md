## 2. SCRIPT (8 slides)

1. THE PLANK TEST. "How long can you hold a plank on your knees? Try it, then swipe."
2. HOW TO TEST. "Forearms down, knees down, body in one line. Count the seconds you can hold it."
3. UNDER 10 SECONDS. "Start here. Wall Plank. 20 seconds. Hands on the wall, walk your feet back, hold."
4. 10 TO 24. "Knee Plank. 25 seconds. Forearms and knees down, body in one line, hold."
5. 25 OR MORE. "Full Plank. 30 seconds. Forearms down, knees off the floor, body in one line."
6. FULL PLANK FOR 30. "Plank Shoulder Tap. 10 reps. High plank on your hands, tap one shoulder at a time."
7. RETEST. "Do your level every day. Take the plank test again in two weeks."
8. YOUR TURN. "How many seconds did you hold?"

Slides 3 to 6 are the results, easiest to hardest. Each footer says when to
move on, so the deck is a test and a ladder at once. The RETEST footer is the
honest line: the wall and shoulder tap versions load the wrists, the
forearm ones do not. Nothing is called easy on the wrists.

## 3. VISUALS / SCENE BREAKDOWN

Typography only, gold poster cover. The figure appears on the Full Plank
slide, the only approved export in the deck. Every movement is in
`movement-library.md` at its library dose: Wall Plank (core, tier 1, wall,
20 second hold), Knee Plank (core, tier 3, 25 second hold), Full
Plank (core, tier 4, 30 second hold), Plank Shoulder Tap (core, tier 5,
10 reps). The test position is the library's Knee Plank (tier 3). The library
lists wrists as a load for Wall Plank and Plank Shoulder Tap
and not for Knee Plank or Full Plank, which is where the honest line comes
from. No health or longevity claim: the test is a starting point, not a score.

## 4. CAPTION AND HASHTAGS

How long can you hold a plank on your knees? Count the seconds, then find your level: Wall Plank, Knee Plank, Full Plank or Plank Shoulder Tap. Save this and retest in two weeks.
